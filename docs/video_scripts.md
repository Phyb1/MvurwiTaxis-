MvurwiTaxis — Video Scripts
Word-for-word scripts to pair with the shot lists in mvurwitaxis-video-concepts.md. Written in English — swap in your own Shona phrasing wherever it'll land better with drivers (you know that better than I do); a couple of natural spots are marked. Read as voiceover, or drop straight on screen as text if you'd rather not talk on camera.

1. "This is what your customers see"
(0–3s, homepage loading) — no voiceover, just text on screen: "Taxis available in Mvurwi"

(3–15s, scrolling listings, tapping one)

"Passengers open this and see drivers, right now. One tap — straight into WhatsApp."

(15–25s, text on screen)

"Free to list. You only pay 20 cents — if we send YOU a customer."

(25–35s, signup page filling in)

"Two minutes. That's it."

(end card, 3s hold)

"Sign up free — link below."

2. "One link instead of retyping every day"
(open, screenshot of a typical "available 24/7" post)

"Sound familiar? Posting this every day, in every group?" (natural spot for Shona: something close to "Chero nguva unenge uchidaro" — "you do this all the time")

(cut to MvurwiTaxis profile page)

"Post it once. It stays up — passengers find you without you lifting a finger."

(end card)

"Free listing. Link below."

3. Driver testimonial
No script — this one works because it's unscripted. Ask the driver three questions on camera and keep whichever 10–15 seconds is most honest:

"How did you hear about MvurwiTaxis?"
"Did you get a customer from it?"
"Would you tell another driver to join?"
Don't feed them lines — a slightly rough, real answer outperforms a polished one here.

4. "What's the real price to Harare?"
(0–5s, the rank argument)

"Ever argued about the price to Harare?"

(5–20s, scrolling the fare board)

"MvurwiTaxis has the real prices. No guessing."

(20–30s, end card)

"Check the fare board — link below."

5. "Request Any Taxi" demo
(0–5s, passenger tapping the button)

"Waiting at the roadside? Just tap 'Request Any Taxi.'"

(5–20s, split to driver's phone getting notified, then replying)

"Every driver who's online gets notified — first to reply gets the customer."

(20–30s, end card)

"No waiting around. Link below."

General delivery notes
Keep each line under ~4 seconds spoken — matches the fast cuts in the shot list, and it's what holds attention on Shorts.
If recording voiceover separately from the screen recording, record 2–3 takes of each line — the flat, slightly-too-fast first take is usually more natural than the "performed" third one.
For the testimonial (#3), film horizontal-safe-for-vertical (keep the driver centered) so you can crop to 9:16 without losing them from frame.
