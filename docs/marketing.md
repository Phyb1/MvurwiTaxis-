MvurwiTaxis — Marketing Strategy
The core problem
The code — search, fare board, "Request Any Taxi", instant WhatsApp connect — is the actual pitch. Nobody buys it from a description; they buy it from seeing it work. So every tactic below has one job: get a driver or passenger to tap the link and see something real, not to explain the platform to them.

That means sequencing matters more than volume: an empty directory kills trust on first tap, and a directory with no passenger requests looks pointless to a driver. Drivers first, always — a few sections below explain why each channel exists and when to lean on it.

Phase 1 — Drivers (now, before any passenger push)
Goal: 15–20 real, listed drivers before a single passenger-facing post goes out. 5+ of them Verified.

There's no official rank in Mvurwi, so "walk up and sign them up in person" isn't available here — the substitute is running four channels in parallel and letting each feed the others.

Channel 1 — WhatsApp DM (reply to their own "taxi available" posts)
Every driver posting "available 24/7, call/app" in a group is already advertising — you're offering a better channel for the same thing, not a cold pitch. Use the templates already drafted (Template A/B/C + follow-up) — lead with "Hi, it's Phyb from MvurwiTaxis," link straight to signup, no back-and-forth DM needed.

Channel 2 — Calls
For leads that go quiet on text, or as the first touch when you'd rather move fast: same 2-sentence pitch as an in-person script — what it is, that it's free, offer to fill in the signup form yourself while they talk. End every call asking who else they know — this is your cheapest referral source.

Channel 3 — Referrals
Ask every signed-up driver directly who else they know, rather than waiting for it to come up. A small, concrete incentive (e.g. priority Pro pricing for 2 successful referrals) turns a passive "sure, I know a few guys" into them actually messaging those guys today. Ask where referred drivers work from — that tells you where flyers are actually landing.

Channel 4 — Fuel station flyers
Put the fare board on the flyer, not just the signup link — fare arguments are what gets a flyer read. Lead with the free-tier line ("Free to list — you only pay if we send you a customer") since a 5-second glance won't get them to click through and find that out themselves. Where possible, leave a few with the attendant directly rather than just posting one — attendants see the same drivers daily and can hand one over personally, which is closer to a referral than a passive poster.

Tracking (once running 4 channels at once)
A simple source column — WhatsApp text / call / referral / flyer — against each lead. After a couple of weeks this tells you which channel is actually converting, not just which one feels busiest.

Verified badges as the multiplier
Verify (license/vehicle reg) as fast as you get each driver signed up. Verified badges are the trust signal that pulls in the next wave of drivers without you doing anything — this is the compounding effect that makes the first 15–20 the hard part and everything after easier.

Phase 2 — Passengers (only once the directory looks alive)
Trigger to start: drivers are actually listed and a few are Verified. Not before — a directory with 3 drivers looks dead to the first passenger who checks.

WhatsApp Status + groups — one post per group (community, church, school-parent, vendor), not a campaign. Repeated posting burns trust faster than it builds reach in a town this size. Rotate between the recruiting hook, the fare-board proof, and the "Request Any Taxi" value prop (templates already drafted).
Fare board as evergreen content — "what's the real price to Harare?" is a recurring argument; screenshot the Fares page and post it whenever that conversation happens organically. Reusable indefinitely, unlike a one-off announcement.
Driver-as-marketer — once a driver's profile is worth sharing, their one-tap "share this profile" button is free distribution you don't have to run. Only works after Phase 1 — there has to be something real on the other end of the link.
Physical flyer — fare board + URL at 2–3 high-traffic spots (shop counter, church noticeboard, wherever drivers/passengers actually gather, given there's no rank). Cheap, and word-of-mouth off one flyer travels fast at this scale.
Phase 3 — Convert free drivers to Pro (ongoing, ambient)
Don't pitch Pro until a driver has felt at least one real free-tier lead land — earlier than that it reads as a paywall, not a value prop. After their 2nd–3rd hot lead in a month (approaching the free cap), message directly and specifically: "You're close to your free lead limit — Pro is $3/week and leads on top of that are free." Track this by eye (driver list sorted by leads used) — no need to automate at current volume.

What to watch (in priority order)
Driver retention, not signups — "went online in the last 7 days" over "signed up ever." A driver who never goes online isn't a real driver.
Leads per online driver — if online drivers aren't getting leads, that's a passenger-awareness gap, not a reason to recruit more drivers.
Free-to-Pro conversion — the actual revenue engine, watch monthly.
Manual confirmation load — should trend down as Pro adoption grows.
What not to do
No paid ads — Mvurwi's whole addressable market is small enough that free channels (WhatsApp, calls, referrals, flyers, word of mouth) cover it.
Don't discount the 20c hot-lead price to "grow faster" — the bottleneck is awareness, not price resistance.
Don't start Phase 2 early. An empty-looking directory is the single biggest risk to the whole push.
